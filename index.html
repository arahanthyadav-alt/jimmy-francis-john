The Journey

A decade of finding the note that stays.

2016 — 2018
The band years begin

Jimmy joins Pineapple Express as vocalist. The band's debut EP Uplift lands in 2018 — four tracks of Carnatic-prog alchemy, covered by Rolling Stone India and carried across college festival stages.

2020
Songs against the silence

When lockdown empties the stages, Pineapple Express builds anyway — the EP Passages is recorded entirely under lockdown, earning the band its second Rolling Stone India feature.

2022
“Nila” changes everything

A quiet collaboration with Keethan and violinist Shravan Sridhar — written for three nephews, hoping the next generation is kinder to itself. It becomes a phenomenon: ten million streams and counting.

2023 — 2024
The catalog deepens

“Vaan,” “Ariya Theeram,” “Kadhali,” “Arike,” “Aasmaan” — a run of singles across four languages, each one widening the circle of listeners who know his voice before they know his name.

2025
New colors

“Hridaya” in Kannada, “Chekkeru Nee” with flutist Arjun MPN, “Dil” in Hindi — proof that the voice travels anywhere feeling lives.

2026
Now playing

“En Kadhali” and its k. remix open the newest chapter, while Jimmy fronts Bryden-Parth as lead singer — carrying the songs from your headphones to the live stage. The best ones, he'd tell you, are still ahead.

The Story

Some voices perform.
This one confides.

Jimmy Francis John doesn't chase the spotlight — the songs find him. Rooted in Kerala and forged on Bengaluru's stages, he lives between worlds: the roar of a prog-fusion band in full flight, and the hush of an acoustic single recorded like a late-night conversation.

“The best songs don't ask to be heard. They ask to be kept.”

You may have met his voice without knowing his name — in “Nila,” the lullaby streamed more than ten million times; in the wandering warmth of “Ariya Theeram”; on festival stages with Pineapple Express, where Carnatic melody meets distortion and neither blinks.

Five languages. Three bands — today he's the lead voice of Bryden-Parth. One instinct: a catalog built not on volume but on feeling — every release a small room you're invited into. Step inside. Stay a while.
